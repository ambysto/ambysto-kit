Ambysto Optimer

Which app. Which number.

One row per app for memory, CPU and disk. Pressure, swap and heat sit above them. Cleanup measures first, removes caches that grow back, and asks before anything personal.

  • Live memory, CPU and disk, per app.
  • A floating bar that stays on screen.
  • Measure, then clean only caches that rebuild.
  • Personal files stay unticked until you tick them.
  • It never force-quits a process.

Ambysto Lean

Where the disk went.

The map, the large files, the old files, the byte-identical copies, and the leftovers of an app you removed. Only the rows you tick move to the Trash.

  • A map of the whole disk.
  • Large files, old files, and exact duplicates.
  • Uninstall an app, then the files it left behind.
  • Every move is checked, then written down.
  • Put it back from the journal.

Ambysto DevBox

What this Mac is running.

The port, the process, and the project it belongs to. The disk your containers keep. The coding agents that are busy. Stopping asks you first.

  • Every listener: port, process, project, and how far it is reachable.
  • Containers, and the disk the runtime is holding.
  • Agent sessions, with CPU and memory.
  • A runaway session is flagged in the list.
  • Stop, only after you confirm.

Ambysto Power

The battery, and what spends it.

Charge, draw, time left, health and cycles. Which apps use the power. How hot the Mac is. Who is keeping it awake. It measures. It does not force a mode.

  • Charge, watts, time remaining, health and cycle count.
  • Power by app.
  • Thermal state, on the same Mac.
  • The list of what is keeping the Mac awake.
  • Keep-awake you start yourself, with the screen on, and a way to stop it.

Ambysto Tune

A switch. And the way back.

Finder, Dock, keyboard, login items, privacy and Spotlight. Each switch stores the value it replaced. One click puts that value back. Restore all puts the Mac back.

  • Every tweak is a switch, with the effect written under it.
  • The previous value is saved before anything changes.
  • Restore one tweak, or every tweak.
  • Login items and background items, in the same window.
  • A journal of what changed on this Mac.

Ambysto Gate

Which app. Which address.

Outgoing connections, decided when each one starts. You see the app and where it is going. You allow it or you block it. The choice is per app, and you can undo it.

  • WatchSee every app and where it connects. Nothing is blocked until you say so.
  • AskA new destination asks you. Allow it, or block it, for that app.
  • LockdownOnly what you have already allowed gets out. The rest waits for you.
  • Rules for an app, including lists and timed rules.
  • A journal of what you decided, with undo.
  • History of the activity, on this Mac.

Questions.

Why seven apps?

Each one does one job, and that job stays free. One key covers Pro in every app. They are not modules of a cleaner.

Why not the Mac App Store?

The sandbox blocks the work: reading another app’s memory, cleaning caches, writing another app’s settings. The apps are offered direct.

Does Pro lock the cleanup, the delete, or the restore?

No. Free keeps the job. Pro adds a longer history, export, alerts, and presets.

What does the key do?

It is checked on the Mac, with no network call. It names the apps it covers. Registered to the name on the key. It is not a subscription.

Windows?

A native Windows app, built from the same written behavior. The Mac apps come first. No phone app.
